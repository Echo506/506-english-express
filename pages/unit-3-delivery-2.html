ACTIVIDAD 01 · DIÁLOGO

Marcus Jackson

🎷

Barbara está en Nueva Orleans y conversa con Kevin Ryan sobre la vida de un destacado músico de jazz.

🔊 Audio sugerido: assets/audio/unit-3/a-life-marcus-jackson.mp3

Kevin: O.K., Barbara. Where do you want to begin?

Barbara: With Marcus Jackson. He was brilliant. I heard him play the saxophone in Chicago. I'll never forget it.

Kevin: O.K. Let's see... I have his biography here...

Kevin: Marcus Jackson. He was born in a small town in southern Georgia. He came from a poor family: his father was a farm worker, and Marcus was the youngest of ten children.

Kevin: He had very little schooling, and left home when he was fifteen. Later he went to San Francisco, where he joined the navy.

Barbara: When did he start playing jazz?

Kevin: In the early fifties. He traveled to Japan and lived in Yokohama for four years. He played in a small bar there, and became quite well known.

Idea principal: Marcus Jackson nació en Georgia en una familia pobre. Dejó su casa a los quince años, ingresó a la marina y más tarde viajó a Japón, donde comenzó a hacerse conocido como músico de jazz.

ACTIVIDAD 02 · DIÁLOGO

Éxito y legado

💿

Kevin explica los años finales de Marcus Jackson y le recomienda a Barbara visitar un bar histórico en Nueva Orleans.

🔊 Audio sugerido: assets/audio/unit-3/a-life-blue-lagoon.mp3

Kevin: He came back to the States in 1954 and met up with Boots Robinson. They made their first record in 1955, which was a big hit.

Barbara: Didn't he also play with Bobby Lee for a while?

Kevin: Yes, and they got married in 1960. They didn't have any children. They settled here in New Orleans, and then moved to New York, where they lived until Marcus' death in 1963.

Barbara: Where did he play in New Orleans?

Kevin: In a little bar in the French Quarter. It's got a great atmosphere. It's just like it was forty years ago.

Barbara: Good. I'll go there tomorrow night. Where is it?

Kevin: It's in a small alley off Bourbon Street. It's called “The Blue Lagoon”.

Barbara: Great.

Kevin: Before you go, take these records. Listen to them. I think you'll like them.

Idea principal: Marcus regresó a Estados Unidos en 1954, grabó un éxito en 1955, se casó en 1960 y vivió entre Nueva Orleans y Nueva York hasta su muerte en 1963. Barbara visitará The Blue Lagoon.

ACTIVIDAD 06 · GRAMÁTICA

Verbos irregulares y did

✍️

Algunos verbos irregulares

They are making a record. → They made a record.

He comes from Georgia. → He came from Georgia.

I am getting married. → I got married.

Preguntas con did

He traveled to Japan. → Did he travel to Japan?

Después de did, el verbo vuelve a su forma base: Did he travel?, no Did he traveled?.

ACTIVIDAD 07 · GRAMÁTICA

Didn't y for

⏳

Oraciones negativas en pasado

I heard him play the saxophone, not the violin. → I didn't hear him play the violin.

Con didn't, usamos el verbo base: didn't hear, didn't have, didn't travel.

Duración con for

She lived in the States from 1986 to 1988. → She lived in the States for two years.

Marcus lived in Yokohama for four years.

ACTIVIDAD 08 · GRAMÁTICA

Ago y which

🔗

Tiempo con ago

I went there at 3:00 p.m. Two hours. → I was there two hours ago.

Unir ideas con which

They made a record. It was a big hit. → They made a record which was a big hit.

En este tipo de oración, that puede sustituir a which.

ACTIVIDAD 09 · CULTURA

Jazz y Nueva Orleans

🎺

Una tradición musical

Nueva Orleans se asocia estrechamente con la historia del jazz. Su ubicación junto al río Mississippi, la mezcla de comunidades y las tradiciones musicales locales contribuyeron al desarrollo de estilos que influyeron en músicos de todo Estados Unidos.

El French Quarter es una de las zonas más conocidas de la ciudad. Sus calles, arquitectura, música y festividades, como Mardi Gras, forman parte de la imagen cultural de Nueva Orleans.

El jazz se caracteriza por la improvisación, la exploración musical y la interacción entre músicos. Su evolución se relaciona con ciudades como Nueva Orleans y Chicago.
